## 4. Findings

### F1 — Zero-rejecting nonconstant coefficients leak below-threshold information

**Severity:** blocking cryptographic design
**Location:** `src/cold/shamir.js:195-198` and coefficient construction around `src/cold/shamir.js:253-256`.
**Observed:** `randomFieldElement()` repeatedly samples until the field value is non-zero, and every nonconstant polynomial coefficient uses it. For threshold 2 over GF(2^3), with observed `x=1`, `y=5`, restricting `a` to 1..7 makes possible secrets `0,1,2,3,4,6,7`; secret `5` is impossible because it would require `a=0`. One share therefore excludes one field value. The information reduction is `log2(8/7) ≈ 0.192645` bits per GF(8) segment in this minimal demonstration. The packet independently acknowledges that the distribution is not the full uniform coefficient space and that intended information-theoretic secrecy cannot honestly be claimed.
**Expected:** If P2.4 claims threshold secrecy in the ordinary information-theoretic sense, fewer than threshold shares must not narrow the secret distribution through an avoidable coefficient-space restriction.
**Required action:** Obtain the maintainer cryptographic design decision already identified in the packet, record it in the ADR, implement the chosen compatibility/secrecy policy, and add independent vectors/tests for that exact policy. Do not casually change the sampler without resolving compatibility and degree semantics.
