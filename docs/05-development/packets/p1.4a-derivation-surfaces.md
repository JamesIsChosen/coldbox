# P1.4a — Derivation paths and address derivation surfaces

**Branch:** `p1.4a-derivation-surfaces`
**Base:** `ui.10b-workstation-shell` at `d02e48c2fefa4b0c3404a6e92993a7c39fda27c8` — **not `main`**. See §2.1; this is the first thing to check before reading anything else.
**Head:** `185e4cd4f1bb3ece0bc6e82522c74416ce58470a`
**Roadmap item:** [P1.4a](../ROADMAP.md) · *Deps: P1.4, P1.5*
**Marker:** `[~]`. One acceptance criterion is **not met** and is reported as such in §4.

---

## 1. Summary

P1.4 and P1.5 built the derivation engine; no item ever owned its user-facing surfaces, so the only route to it was indirect, through Verify Bench, and the shell was marking two destinations unavailable against two completed items. This adds the sealed **Derive** workspace with **Derivation paths** and **Address derivation**, both lenses on the focused released secret under [ADR-0045](../adr/0045-released-secret-model.md), both calling the existing `src/cold/derivation.js` engine.

## 2. Scope

**In:**

- A seventh sealed workspace, `#cold-group-derive`, holding `#cold-derivation-paths` and `#cold-address-derivation`.
- Three public wrappers on the derivation engine (`bitcoinAccountPath`, `evmAccountPath`, `coinType`) so the surfaces read the purpose and coin-type facts from the engine instead of copying them.
- A chain catalogue gated on independently recorded test vectors.
- Navigation: the sealed rail, the sealed mobile More sheet, the sealed hub, and the warm All flows index stop marking the two destinations unavailable.
- `test/p1.4a-derivation-surfaces.test.js` and a `verifyDerivationSurfaces` browser scenario.
- Docs: a three-depth guide, SPEC §12.4, a pointer from the derivation-paths reference, CHANGELOG, roadmap marker `[~]`.

**Deliberately not in:** descriptors (P4.9), child seeds (P4.6), wallet-scoped address lifecycle (WAL.4), the custom chain registry itself (P4.2), watch-only xpub entry on these surfaces, and any "add to Registry" action — none is claimed by this item's acceptance.

**Touched outside the item, and why:**

- `test/ui.4-sealed-realm.test.js` asserted exactly six sealed groups. There are now seven. The assertion was renamed and widened to the invariant UI.4 actually owns — that the groups are declared once, hub-linked in order, and the cold CSP is unchanged by any restructure.
- `scripts/run-browser-harness.js`: the group counts (6→7), the focus-indicator counts (6→8, two sites), the cold More-sheet expectations, and UI.10b's assertion that both P1.4a entries are `aria-disabled`. Every one of these was correct when written and became false when this item built the destinations. **This class of change was found by running the whole harness, not the new scenario** — see §9.
- `src/index.html`: two disabled buttons in the All flows index became links, matching the sibling sealed entries.

### 2.1 Why this is stacked on `ui.10b-workstation-shell`

P1.4a's declared deps are P1.4 and P1.5, both `[x]` on `main`. Its acceptance, however, requires the destinations to be reachable from "the approved rail, the mobile More sheet and the All flows index". **The All flows index does not exist on `main`** — it is the `#advanced` page UI.10b adds — and the approved rail and realm-aware More sheet are likewise UI.10b deliverables. UI.10b's branch already lists both destinations as unavailable, owned by `P1.4a`, which is where this item came from.

Building on `main` would therefore have failed the criterion by construction. The maintainer chose stacking over reordering the roadmap. Consequences a reviewer should hold:

- **This branch cannot merge before UI.10b passes review.** Its diff is against UI.10b's head, not `main`.
- UI.10b is itself in progress and unreviewed (its packet describes passes 1 and 3 of 3). If UI.10b's shell changes, this branch needs a rebase and a re-run.
- The base SHA is pinned above. If `ui.10b-workstation-shell` has moved since, that is the first thing to reconcile.

### 4.1 The unmet criterion

**The parity criterion is not satisfied and this item does not claim it.**

[ui-parity.md §6.1](../../01-spec/ui-parity.md#61-product-identity-redesign-before-ui11-resumes) pauses new pixel-convergence work until the replacement design is implemented through UI.10a/UI.10b, and UI.11 — which owns the comparison harness, the state matrix and the zero-unexpected-pixel rule — is explicitly gated on UI.10a and UI.10b both being `[x]`. UI.10b is neither merged nor reviewed. There is no committed parity harness to run these two destinations through, and the surrounding shell they would be compared inside is itself unfinished.

Under §2 of the contract these two destinations *inherit* the rolling-surface obligation: when the parity harness exists, they are `PARITY` rows and must match. What I have done instead is build them from the existing sealed design-system components and floors, which is the closest honest approximation available — and said so here rather than describing it as parity.

**A reviewer following review-protocol.md should treat this as a finding.** It is reported rather than hidden because an unverifiable criterion cannot be passed, and because the alternative — quietly reading the criterion as "looks consistent with the other panels" — is the reinterpretation AGENTS.md §7 forbids. Whether the item can close with this criterion deferred to UI.11 is a maintainer decision, not mine.

## 5. Security impact

| Surface | Touched? |
|---|---|
| Realm boundary | **No.** No new message type in either direction; no existing payload changed. Every derivation happens inside the sealed frame and its results are rendered there. |
| Message schema | **No.** `architecture.md`'s tables are unchanged. |
| CSP | **No.** Both policies byte-identical; the existing test asserting the cold policy string passes unchanged. |
| Vault format | **No.** Nothing here reads or writes a compartment. |
| Derivation | **Yes**, but additively: three wrappers over existing internals. No derivation algorithm changed. |
| Randomness | **No.** Nothing here generates a secret. |
| `connect-src` | **No new host.** |

**If this implementation is wrong, what does an attacker gain?**

Not exfiltration — the boundary is untouched and the negative tests cover it from both sides. The realistic damage is **a user acting on a wrong address**: deriving from the wrong path and believing the resulting address belongs to them, or comparing against a hardware wallet and reaching a false conclusion. That is why the browser scenario compares against published BIP-84 vectors end to end through the real UI rather than asserting the engine in isolation — the engine was already proven; what was unproven was whether the surface feeds it the right arguments and renders the right path beside the right address.

The second failure mode is a chain deriving addresses with no vector backing it. That is what the catalogue guard exists for, and the negative test is the part of this PR I would re-read first.

**What I am not certain about:** whether `deriveNodeProjection`'s `network` argument matters for the generic-mode display. It selects version bytes for the returned extended public key, so a generic path under a testnet selection would render a `tpub`-family key. Generic mode is currently pinned to `mainnet` in the catalogue, so this cannot presently be reached — but it is an assumption I made rather than a fact the spec settles. Recorded in §8.

## 8. Assumptions made

Each of these is a decision the spec does not settle. None touches a security boundary, the vault format, or a cryptographic choice; where one came close I stopped and asked instead (§2.1).

1. **"The All flows index" means UI.10b's `#advanced` page.** Nothing else in the repository is called that. This is the assumption the whole branch base rests on; if it is wrong, §2.1 is wrong.
2. **The two flow-index entries link to `#cold-realm-status`**, matching every other sealed entry in that index, rather than deep-linking into the frame. A cross-realm deep link would need the `ui.navigate` message, and adding boundary traffic for a convenience is not something I will do without a decision.
3. **Derive is the fourth workspace, making seven.** The approved navigation taxonomy orders the cold groups Seeds & lineage / Forge / Derive / Split & carry / Recover & verify / Session, so Derive sits after Seed Forge. Consequence: the "Workspace N of 6" eyebrows renumbered and UI.4's count assertion changed (§2).
4. **The address count defaults to 5, not the engine's 20.** Two chains rendered at once makes 20 into 40 rows. The engine's 1..1000 bounds are unchanged and enforced by the engine, not by the UI.
5. **Generic mode is pinned to `mainnet`** for extended-key version bytes. See the uncertainty in §5.
6. **Generic mode shows public node metadata rather than an address.** Tier 3 has no address encoding by definition, so a path with nothing rendered from it would be a validator, not a derivation surface.
7. **The arbitrary path field is enabled only in generic mode**, reading the criterion literally ("enter an arbitrary path in the P1.5 generic mode").
8. **Bitcoin address paths are composed as `<accountPath>/<change>/<index>` for display.** The engine returns addresses without paths for Bitcoin (its EVM helper does return them). Composition is presentation, not a second derivation — and the unit test re-derives each composed path independently and checks it produces the address shown beside it, so a wrong composition cannot pass.
9. **`testVectors` is a non-empty string naming the source.** A boolean would have been a claim with nothing behind it; a reader can check the named vectors.

## 9. What to scrutinise

Where I am least confident, most important first.

1. **The base branch (§2.1).** Everything else is downstream of that decision. If a reviewer disagrees that P1.4a depends on UI.10b, this branch is on the wrong base and the packet's parity discussion changes.
2. **The unmet parity criterion (§4.1).** I am reporting a criterion as not met and asking for a maintainer decision. Check that I have not quietly satisfied myself with something weaker.
3. **The custom-chain guard.** It is the criterion with the least existing machinery behind it — P4.2 does not exist yet, so the guard protects a future. Ask whether a filter over a frozen literal is a real gate or a comment with a test attached. My view: it is real, because it is the *only* path from catalogue to selection, and the negative test injects an unbacked entry rather than trusting the shipped list. But it is the piece most likely to be argued with.
4. **My edits to other items' assertions (§2).** Four separate assertions belonging to UI.4 and UI.10b now say something different. Each is a place where an item made a claim that this item made false, and each is a place where a lazy fix would be to loosen an assertion until it passes. Check that I widened them to the invariant rather than weakened them — the UI.4 one in particular went from "six groups" to "the declared groups, hub-linked in order", which is a rename as well as a change.
5. **`updateDerivationControls` call sites.** The panels' enabled state depends on `vaultCryptoReady` and the focused secret. I wired it into bootstrap completion, focus change, registry clear, and the consumer refresh. If there is a fifth path that changes either input and does not call it, a control could be stale. I did not find one; I am not certain there isn't one.
6. **The `else` branch I added to the `data-cold-more-target` handler.** It focuses the target for any id not in the existing three-way chain. Today only my two destinations reach it. A future entry gets that behaviour by default, which may or may not be what its author wants.
7. **Bundle cost.** +46KB for two panels is more than I expected (§11).

## 10. Self-assessment

**What might be wrong with this?**

The generic-mode network pin (§5) is the most likely place for a latent wrong answer, and it is unreachable today, which is exactly the kind of thing that becomes reachable later without anyone rechecking it.

Beyond that: I verified in one browser engine. The two defects I found — a 19px touch target and a rail link that navigated nowhere — were both found by the browser, not the suite, and both would have passed a green 480-test run. I have no reason to think Firefox differs, and no evidence that it doesn't.

**What I did not do that arguably should have been done:**

- No Firefox run, and no physical device (§7).
- I did not fix Verify Bench's 36.8px controls or the other cold rail links' bare fragment hrefs, both of which I found while working (§6). Fixing them would have put two more items in one branch. They are not filed as roadmap items — I do not have the authority to add items to the roadmap for work nobody scoped, so they are recorded here and in the handoff for the maintainer to file.
- I did not add an ADR. My reading is that no structural decision was made — the released-secret model, the boundary and the engine's contract are all unchanged, and this item adds surfaces to an existing architecture. A reviewer who thinks the chain catalogue and its vector gate constitute a structural decision would be entitled to disagree, and that is a defensible reading.

**Known limitations shipping with this change:**

- Seed-derived only. No watch-only xpub entry on these surfaces, though the engine supports it.
- No "add to Registry" action from a derived address; that is warm-side registry work.
- Bitcoin and EVM only, plus generic. Every other chain in SPEC §12.1 remains engine-level.

**Follow-up this creates:** the two pre-existing defects in §6; watch-only entry on these surfaces; and the parity certification of both destinations when UI.11 resumes.
